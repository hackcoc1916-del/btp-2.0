# Stage 4: Cross-Dataset Generalization Report

## 1. Executive Summary

The Stage 4 analysis evaluates the cross-dataset transferability of Network Intrusion Detection System (NIDS) models trained on the **CICIDS2017** dataset. Models were tested against two completely unseen environments: **CSE-CIC-IDS2018** and **Lycos-Unicas-IDS2018**. 

This evaluation quantified domain shift, measured performance degradation, and ranked models based on their robustness to novel network conditions.

### Key Findings
1. **XGBoost is the Most Robust Model**: Across all evaluations (binary and multiclass across both external datasets), XGBoost maintained the highest robustness score, followed closely by LightGBM.
2. **Binary Classification is Vastly Superior for Generalization**: While multiclass models failed completely (F1 plummeting to ~0.20), binary models (BENIGN vs ATTACK) retained significant performance (XGBoost Binary F1 > 0.86 on both datasets).
3. **Severe Domain Shift Exists**: Statistical analysis revealed massive feature drift between CICIDS2017 and external datasets, with over half the features experiencing HIGH drift (PSI > 0.25).
4. **Zero-Day Attacks Evade Detection**: When presented with truly novel attacks ('OTHER' in CIC2018), all models failed with a 0% detection rate, confidently misclassifying the malicious traffic as BENIGN.

---

## 2. Research Questions (RQs) Answered

### RQ4.1: How severe is domain shift between CICIDS2017 and external datasets?
**Severe.** The Population Stability Index (PSI) and Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests demonstrate catastrophic distribution drift:
* **CIC2018:** 42 out of 81 features showed HIGH drift severity. Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) was 0.0286.
* **Lycos:** 48 out of 81 features showed HIGH drift severity. JSD was 0.0314.

### RQ4.2: Which model generalizes best to unseen network environments?
**XGBoost.** It outperformed all classical tree-based models and Logistic Regression.
* **Winner:** XGBoost (Robustness Score: 0.4869)
* **Runner-Up:** LightGBM (Robustness Score: 0.4208)
* The gradient boosting frameworks demonstrated a clear advantage in adapting to shifted data manifolds compared to Random Forest and Extra Trees, which catastrophically collapsed outside the training distribution.

### RQ4.3: Does binary classification improve cross-dataset transfer?
**Yes, drastically.** 
* **Multiclass XGBoost** average F1 dropped to **0.27**.
* **Binary XGBoost** average F1 remained at **0.899**.
* Grouping all attacks into a single "ATTACK" manifold forces the model to learn broader anomalies rather than brittle, attack-specific signatures that change between network topologies.

### RQ4.4: Which attack families transfer successfully across datasets?
* **DOS/DDoS** and **Brute Force** families transferred with high success when using gradient boosting models.
* Highly specific application-layer attacks (e.g., Web Attacks) failed to transfer effectively, as their feature footprints are heavily tied to the specific training network topology.

### RQ4.5: How much performance is lost when moving from internal to external evaluation?
* **Binary Transfer Loss:** Minimal for advanced models. XGBoost F1 dropped by only **6.33%** on CIC2018 and **13.69%** on Lycos.
* **Multiclass Transfer Loss:** Catastrophic. All models experienced >60% performance degradation, with Random Forest and Extra Trees losing ~80% of their F1 score.

---

## 3. Unseen Attack Analysis (Zero-Day Detection)

An analysis was conducted on how models handle attack labels present in external datasets that were never seen during the CICIDS2017 training phase.

* **Target:** The "OTHER" attack class in CIC2018.
* **Detection Rate:** **0.0%** across all 5 models.
* **Misclassification:** 100% of the unseen attacks were predicted as BENIGN.
* **Confidence:** The models were highly confident in their wrong predictions (XGBoost: 99.1% confidence, LightGBM/Logistic Regression: 100% confidence).

**Conclusion:** Supervised models trained on static network environments are entirely blind to novel attack vectors, highlighting the critical need for hybrid supervised-unsupervised approaches for zero-day threat detection.
